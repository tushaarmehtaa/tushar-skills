# Single-message patterns

This is a heuristic library. The main skill controls intake, safety, output, and verification.

## Message architecture

A strong cold message usually needs three moves:

1. **Relevant observation:** a verified recipient or company signal and why it matters.
2. **Credible connection:** the sender's offer or experience mapped to that situation.
3. **Reasonable next step:** one low-friction ask appropriate to the relationship.

Compress, reorder, or omit a move when the context supports it. Do not force a credential sentence when usefulness or a warm connection is the real reason to reply.

## Signal quality

Prefer signals that are current, specific, and relevant to the ask:

- the recipient's own writing, talk, product decision, or open role;
- an official company launch, filing, release, or documented change;
- a mutual contact the sender is authorized to mention;
- a concrete workflow or artifact the sender genuinely inspected.

Avoid generic admiration, scraped trivia, sensitive personal information, tragedy, or a signal unrelated to the offer. Keep the source and retrieval date outside send-ready copy.

## Ask design

Match the ask to the value and relationship:

- answer one focused question;
- review a small artifact;
- route to the right person;
- take a short call with a reason and flexible scheduling;
- consider a clearly scoped offer.

Do not manufacture a specific time merely to satisfy a formula. A direct yes/no or referral ask may be lower friction.

## Subject and opening defaults

- Make the subject accurate and recognizable in an inbox.
- Prefer specificity over curiosity tricks.
- Never fake a reply/forward thread or imply an existing relationship.
- The opening should lose meaning if pasted to an unrelated recipient.
- Treat character and word limits as mobile/readability checks, not universal success rules.

## Deliverability and compliance check

Before volume, verify sending-domain authentication with the actual provider, mailbox reputation, bounce handling, suppression, opt-out processing, sender identification, list provenance, recipient type, and applicable jurisdictions. Use current provider and regulator documentation. Do not diagnose delivery from open rates alone; privacy features make opens unreliable.

## Audit questions

- Is every claim supportable?
- Is relevance obvious before background?
- Is the message useful or merely personalized?
- Is there one visible next step?
- Could a recipient decline or opt out without friction?
- Does the copy sound appropriate for the sender's real voice and status?
