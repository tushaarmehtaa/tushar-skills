# Evidence and standards

Use this reference before making standards, performance, reading-behavior, or conversion claims. Verify time-sensitive guidance live when it affects a release.

## Maintained sources

- [GOV.UK: identify user needs](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/plan-manage-content/identify-user-needs/) — content should support a real task, based on user evidence rather than a solution invented first.
- [GOV.UK design principles](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/government-design-principles) — start with user needs, use data, do less, and remain consistent without forcing uniformity.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: how users read on the web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/) — foundational usability research on scanning, concise structure, and credibility cues. Treat the historical percentages as study findings, not universal current rates.
- [MECLABS research catalog](https://www.meclabs.com/misc/Research_Catalog_FULL_6-6-12.pdf) — archived controlled tests showing that page changes require experiments; do not generalize a reported lift to another product or audience.
- [web.dev: optimize LCP](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp) — current LCP threshold and diagnostic subparts. The good threshold is 2.5 seconds or less at the 75th percentile, segmented by device; a single local run does not establish field performance.
- [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) — normative accessibility criteria for contrast, reflow, input, focus, motion, and other requirements.

## How to use research

Separate four evidence classes:

1. **Standard:** a normative requirement such as a WCAG success criterion.
2. **Observed product evidence:** analytics, research, recordings, support questions, and controlled tests from this product.
3. **External research:** a transferable hypothesis whose population and method must be considered.
4. **Heuristic:** experienced judgment to test, not a guaranteed conversion law.

Never present a benchmark, historical experiment, or expert preference as a universal conversion rate or mandatory page pattern.
