I'd hold off on the $2.5M seed in January. Fix your runway now, spend roughly November to March testing whether Harborline can grow into a venture-sized company, and decide in March. If the evidence is there, raise in Q2 from a stronger position. The full plan is in `raise-plan.md`.

**Why not January:**
- **The market is small at today's price.** Every independent US bike shop paying $99/month adds up to about $4.75M a year in revenue. With Canada and the UK it's about $7.7M, and that assumes you sign every shop. A seed investor needs a path to a much bigger company.
- **You'd be raising with almost no cash left.** At $28k/month burn you'd have about 4.5 months of money on January 1 and run out around May 2027. Rounds often take months, so you'd be negotiating against that deadline.
- **Growth is slowing.** It's dropped from about 10% a month to about 6%. At $99 per shop, the revenue level investors usually expect before the next round would mean signing about 840 shops, roughly 21% of all US bike shops. And salespeople are hard to pay for when each shop brings in $1,188 a year.

**What would make it worth raising for**, each of which needs testing:
- Charging well above $99. The product touches each shop's whole parts budget.
- Earning money from the orders you already send to distributors, through referral fees, a cut of order volume, or financing for shops.
- Showing that ski and outdoor shops can use the same product without much rework.

The plan has pass/fail targets for each test. If two or three pass by March, raise a smaller round sized to a specific next milestone. If not, aim for profitability. That's about 425 shops at today's price and fewer after a price increase. Low churn and 40% referrals make this a good business either way.

**This month:**
- Ask your existing angels for a $250k–400k extension, and/or cut costs.
- Raise the price for new customers.
- Write down the March decision date and the targets you'll judge against.

One open question could change the urgency. I assumed the $28k/month burn already accounts for revenue, because $210k ÷ $28k matches your 7.5 months of runway. If $28k is total spending before revenue, you're actually losing about $14k a month and have about 15 months of runway.

On valuation, I haven't suggested a number. Raising $2.5M for 20% of the company would value it at about 75× this year's revenue, which only a bigger growth story could justify. I also haven't checked any outside data: the 4,000-shop count and the ski/outdoor market sizes are your figures or unverified, and the investor list still needs building and vetting.
