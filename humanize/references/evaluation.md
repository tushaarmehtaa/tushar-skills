# Humanize evaluation and sources

Use this checklist after a substantial edit. Fix failures before returning the work or place consequential unknowns in `Needs you`.

## Voice and meaning

- Can the core point be stated the same way before and after?
- Did distinctive vocabulary, cadence, humor, uncertainty, and personal detail survive?
- Are strong original sentences preserved rather than polished for consistency?
- Did any edit change attribution, causality, chronology, comparison, scope, or confidence?
- Is the amount changed proportional to the diagnosed pattern?

## Nothing invented

- Are there no new facts, statistics, quotations, examples, sources, product abilities, or opinions?
- Are unsupported claims identified rather than quietly strengthened?
- Are factual defects separated from stylistic findings?

## Over-correction

- Does the result avoid uniform sentence length, stacked punch lines, and repeated “plain” sentence shapes?
- Did the edit preserve useful jargon, passive constructions, repetition, and hedges where the format needs them?
- Does the prose sound like the source author rather than the editor's house style?
- For short copy, was output kept short instead of forcing a large editorial report?

## Format test

- Does the piece still accomplish its actual job?
- Is its hierarchy scannable where readers scan and continuous where they need an argument?
- When the format is spoken, does it work aloud?
- When the format is operational, are instructions, labels, and consequences unambiguous?

## Sources and provenance

- [Google developer documentation style guide: active voice](https://developers.google.com/style/voice) — prefer an explicit actor when it improves responsibility and clarity, while retaining passive voice when the actor is irrelevant or intentionally de-emphasized.
- [GOV.UK: identify user needs](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/plan-manage-content/identify-user-needs/) — content should help a defined person complete a real task and should be grounded in evidence.
- [Mailchimp voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/) — stable voice can adapt its tone to the reader's situation; clarity outranks forced entertainment.
- [George Orwell, “Politics and the English Language”](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/) — useful pressure toward concrete, economical language, including the crucial permission to break a rule before producing worse prose.
