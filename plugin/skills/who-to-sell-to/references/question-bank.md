# Question bank

Ask one question per turn, in stage order. Skip anything already answered or found by looking it up. Each question lists the follow-up that matters most and the pattern to look for.

## Stage 1: Paying customers

**Q1. Who has actually paid you in the last 6–12 months, and roughly how much each?**
Real payments only, not pipeline. Rough numbers are fine.
- Follow-up if vague: "Start with the first one. What did you do for them, and did they pay?"
- Look for: concentration (one big customer), and whether revenue comes from one business line or several.

**Q2. For each paying customer: what did you do, what result did they get, and how excited were they?**
Offer a simple scale: very excited (pushed to start, introduced others), polite interest, still deciding.
- Ask one customer per turn if there are several.
- Look for: what they actually bought (it's often narrower or different from what the founder thinks they sell).

**Q3. Did they renew or buy again? If not, why not?**
- Offer the usual reasons: one-off moment ended, no budget, the value wasn't measurable, their priorities changed, the fit was off.
- Look for: one-off vs ongoing value; whether the customer's own situation changed so what you sold stopped mattering to them.

## Stage 2: Triggers and buyers

**Q4. What was happening at each customer right before they bought?**
Prompt with examples: fresh funding, a launch, a key person leaving, expansion, a missed target, a competitor move.
- Look for: triggers that repeat across customers. These become the "when" of the ICP.

**Q5. Which deals didn't close, and why?**
- Look for: deals with no trigger ("nice-to-have"), price objections that are really urgency problems.

**Q6. Who did you talk to in each deal, won and lost, and who approved the money?**
- Look for: founder vs junior contact; whether wins needed a champion who could bring in the decision-maker. Lay won/lost against contact level in a small table.

## Stage 3: Acquisition

**Q7. How did each paying customer first find you, and what was the first small thing they did with you?**
- Look for: the land-and-expand path (event, ticket, small project → bigger deal); warm vs cold; which content or channel created the first contact.

**Q8. Has anyone come to you inbound from your content alone?**
- Look for: whether content creates demand, or only builds trust after another first contact.

## Stage 4: The second ICP (if the business has one)

Many businesses have two buyers: companies and individuals, or users and sponsors. Run this stage for the second one.

**Q9. Have individuals paid for anything? How many, at what price, and who were they?**
Ask for the rough mix: roles, company types, experience levels.

**Q10. What did they want out of it?**
A job, income, clients, a project, learning, status.
- Look for: the gap between the founder's stated promise and what buyers actually came for.

**Q11. What happened to them afterward?**
- Look for: outcome evidence. Without it, mark outcome claims as hypotheses and propose tracking.

## Stage 5: Content

**Q12. What content has actually worked: unusual reach, people at events, a buyer messaging you? What and where?**
- Look for: personal vs brand accounts; social proof formats; which platforms have zero evidence (candidates to cut).

## Stage 6: Goals and capacity

**Q13. What does success look like in 90 days?**
Revenue, number of clients or customers, signups.
- Do the math out loud: implied conversion rates, deal counts, pipeline needed. Call out targets that need a conversion rate far above the evidence, and propose a realistic first milestone.

**Q14. How many hours a week can the team realistically give this, and what's on the calendar that could get in the way?**

## Questions to use when the founder pushes back

- "The contact's seniority doesn't matter, right?" → Show the deal that stalled with a junior contact. The company is the market; the person who owns the budget is why it closes.
- "This is enough context, right?" → Say what's solid and name the two or three missing pieces (usually: who paid and renewed, the trigger, who approved the money).
- "Can't we just say X?" → Ask what evidence supports X today and what would prove it.

## Things to never ask

- Anything you could look up.
- Leading questions ("Wouldn't you say your ICP is…?").
- Hypotheticals about future buying ("Would companies pay for…?"). Ask what already happened.
