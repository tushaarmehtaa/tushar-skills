# People Are the Open Source

## The Eight People Who Became an Industry

In 2017, eight researchers at Google published a paper called "Attention Is All You Need." Every single one of them left the company.

Noam Shazeer co-founded Character.ai, valued in the billions. Aidan Gomez co-founded Cohere, now one of the enterprise LLM leaders. Ashish Vaswani and Niki Parmar co-founded Essential AI. Llion Jones co-founded Sakana AI in Tokyo, building nature-inspired systems. Illia Polosukhin co-founded NEAR Protocol. Jakob Uszkoreit co-founded Inceptive, applying AI to RNA biology. Lukasz Kaiser went to OpenAI.

Google trained those eight people on the hardest problems in AI. Then they scattered across the industry and built companies on exactly what they knew. The architecture stayed at Google. The people did not.

This is not a story about one paper. It is a pattern.

## The OpenAI Exodus

Eighteen startups have been founded by OpenAI alumni as of early 2026. The notable ones:

Anthropic. Dario and Daniela Amodei left OpenAI in 2021 over safety disagreements. Their company is now valued at $380 billion. OpenAI's biggest rival is built by ex-OpenAI people.

SSI. Ilya Sutskever, OpenAI's co-founder and chief scientist, left in 2024, raised $2 billion, now valued at $32 billion. The person who arguably did the most to make GPT-4 work is now building a competitor because he didn't trust his former employer to build AGI safely.

Thinking Machines Lab. Mira Murati, OpenAI's CTO, left in 2024. Emerged from stealth in 2025 valued at $12 billion.

Perplexity. Aravind Srinivas spent a year at OpenAI as a research scientist, left in 2022, built the AI search engine valued at $20 billion.

xAI. Kyle Kosic left OpenAI to co-found Elon Musk's company.

Periodic Labs. Liam Fedus, VP of post-training research at OpenAI, left in 2025, raised $300 million in seed funding from Bezos, Schmidt, and a16z.

The SignalFire data makes the scale visible. Engineers leaving OpenAI for Anthropic do so at a ratio of 8:1. From DeepMind to Anthropic, it is 11:1. Retention rates tell the same story: Anthropic holds 80% of its talent. OpenAI holds 67%. DeepMind holds 78%.

## The DeepMind Drain

Sixteen former DeepMind researchers launched their own ventures in a single recent year. The year before it was seven. The rate is accelerating.

Arthur Mensch left DeepMind and co-founded Mistral AI, now one of Europe's most important labs. David Silver, DeepMind's top reinforcement learning researcher, left to start Ineffable Intelligence. Geoffrey Hinton joined Google when they acquired his startup in 2013, left in 2023 saying he needed freedom to speak about AI risks, and won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2024.

The departures are not quiet. They are structural. When Meta reportedly offered a single AI researcher a $250 million compensation package over four years, it stopped being a talent market and started being something else.

And then there is the reverse acqui-hire. Google hiring Windsurf's CEO and co-founder into DeepMind. Amazon hiring Adept's key members. Microsoft bringing in Inflection AI's team. These are transactions that bypass M&A entirely. Cherry-pick the people, leave the cap table behind.

## What This Actually Means

Patents protect code. Trade secrets protect processes. Nothing protects what is inside a person's head when they walk out the door.

Every time a lab trains a researcher for five years on the hardest problems in AI — scaling, alignment, inference, post-training — they are creating someone who carries that knowledge permanently. The lab might own the weights. They do not own the intuitions, the instincts, the understanding of what works and why.

Anthropic exists because OpenAI trained Dario Amodei. Character.ai exists because Google trained Noam Shazeer. Mistral exists because DeepMind trained Arthur Mensch. The transformer paper exists because Google trained eight people who then scattered across the industry.

The most important thing any AI lab produces is not its models. It is its alumni.

And unlike a published paper, you cannot open-source that strategically. It just happens.

## The Uncomfortable Question

If the people are the open source, then what exactly are the labs protecting when they stop publishing research? The knowledge walks out the door regardless. The only thing secrecy changes is who gets to build on it.

OpenAI used to publish everything. GPT-1, GPT-2, GPT-3 — full papers. GPT-4 came with a technical report that deliberately omitted almost every technical detail. The stated reason was competitive advantage. But the people who built GPT-4 are now spread across four different companies. The details left with them.

The question nobody answers: if the knowledge is walking out the door in people's heads anyway, what is the secrecy actually achieving?
