# Report guide

The report has the sections below, in this order. Each one leads with its point in one sentence, then the table or list that proves it. Short sentences, real numbers, the founder's own names for things. Leave out any section the interview gave no material for.

## Page structure

The ICP card is the hero and stays open, with a "This week" box (three tasks) beside it. Sections 2 to 9 are collapsed rows in the HTML. The row's summary line is the section's conclusion, so a founder who opens nothing still gets the findings. Section 10's checklist is the "This week" box; its open questions are the last row.

## 1. ICP card (required)

The first thing on the page and the part people will screenshot. One sentence that states the ICP, then five lines:

| Field | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| Who | Three or more narrow attributes (stage, type, size, geography, tools in use) |
| When | The triggers that preceded real purchases |
| Buyer | The role that approves money, and the champion path if there is one |
| Replaces | The real alternative: a hire, doing it themselves, another vendor, nothing |
| Not a fit | The two to four disqualifiers learned from lost or churned deals |

Every line must trace to evidence from the interview. If a line rests on a guess, write it and add "(to test)".

## 2. Goals

A small table: goal, target, where it stands today. Include the founder's stated target and, if you called it unrealistic, the realistic milestone next to it.

## 3. Evidence

The deals table: customer, what they are, who we spoke to, trigger, what we did, outcome, lesson. Newest or largest first. A second small table for the second ICP (programs, who came, price, result) if there is one. One line per row, no paragraphs inside cells.

## 4. Patterns

Three to five numbered findings, each one sentence plus the deal that proves it. These are the patterns named during the interview, now final. Example: "Deals closed in under 30 days when the owner was on the call; deals with only a manager took three times longer (three wins vs two stalls)."

## 5. Offer ladder

Entry → core → add-on, with price and purpose for each. Add pricing rules if the interview produced them (anchor, scope-for-discount, billing).

## 6. Sales plan

Pipeline math from the 90-day goal back to a target list size, with the close-rate assumption stated. Lead sources in priority order. The qualification checklist (one line per ICP field). An outreach template that opens with the trigger and proof from a real customer.

## 7. Second ICP and offer

Only if there is one: who they are, the positioning (proven claims only), what's in the offer, pricing, and outcome tracking.

## 8. Content plan

What content is for (its job in the sales motion), a table of channel / audience / what to post / cadence, and rules. Flag unproven channels with a review date.

## 9. Time and scorecard

Hours by area, calendar risks, and the weekly metrics with 90-day targets.

## 10. This week and open questions

Five to eight checkbox tasks for this week, then the open questions the interview couldn't settle.

## Style rules

- Lead every section with its conclusion, never "This section describes…".
- Numbers with units and currency as the founder uses them ($4K, €300, $20/month).
- No adjectives standing in for evidence ("strong", "huge"). Show the number.
- Mark hypotheses as hypotheses.
- Keep the whole report readable in about five minutes.
